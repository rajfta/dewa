### Air-Atomizing / Airspray Coating

In cup guns, compressed air both delivers the paint and atomizes it. The advantages of this method are excellent atomization, perfect paint distribution, and a very smooth finish. The disadvantages are that it requires significant practical skill, high energy consumption, and can strain the operator’s hand joints.

![](/szorasi_tipusok/egy.png)

The image illustrates the top-feed principle. When the trigger is pulled, the needle moves backward, allowing paint to flow toward the nozzle. Here it meets compressed air, which propels the paint through the nozzle, while air exiting the cap holes atomizes it. These guns typically operate at low pressures (2–3 bar).

![](/szorasi_tipusok/ketto.png)

In pressure-feed guns, the paint is moved by a pump or pressure tank, while atomization is still done by air. The advantage is faster and more comfortable operation with relatively good surface quality; the disadvantage is somewhat higher energy consumption.

While top- or bottom-feed guns are suitable for creating high-quality painted surfaces and are mainly used for individual components (e.g., car body panels, furniture finishing), pressure-feed guns also allow small series production with relatively high-quality requirements.

### Airless Coating

As the name suggests, Airless technology does not use air for paint application or atomization. The size, number, and placement of the nozzle orifices determine the droplet size, quality of atomization, and spray pattern width. A high-pressure pump delivers paint to the gun, which exits at 190–400 bar. The paint is forced through the specially shaped nozzle at high speed and power, and no adjustments can be made during spraying.

The advantages are extremely fast application, thick film build, and the ability to spray very viscous coatings. Nozzle availability is extensive, and with a reversible tip, blockages can be cleared in three simple steps: rotate the tip 180°, briefly pull the trigger, and rotate back. Paint can often be sprayed without thinning.

The disadvantages include the need for testing to select the correct nozzle, relatively lower surface finish quality, and the requirement of a high-performance compressor (1–3 m³/min air).

![](/szorasi_tipusok/harom.png)

The top image demonstrates how changing the nozzle orifice size affects film thickness at the same spray pattern, while the second shows how adjusting the spray pattern affects layer thickness with the same orifice size.

This method is suitable for more viscous materials, thicker layers, and applications with lower aesthetic requirements. It is economically viable only in industrial settings, such as steel and construction industries.

### Air-Assisted / Airmix™ Coating

Airmix systems are patented by Sames (other manufacturers refer to this as air-assisted spraying). In this method, the quality of the paint application is primarily determined by the nozzle design, orifice size, and the pressure generated by the material supply pump. Paint is moved by the pump at 60–240 bar, while atomization occurs at the nozzle. The compressed air assists only in adjusting the spray angle within a narrow range, which means that once the correct nozzle is installed, the desired quality can be achieved immediately.

This spraying technology combines the quantity efficiency of Airless with the surface finish quality of Airspray, making it suitable for spraying viscous coatings while producing a significantly more aesthetic surface.

The advantages of this method are that it is very simple and fast to use, the quality depends only slightly on the operator’s experience, and there is a wide range of nozzles available. The disadvantage is that the correct nozzle can only be selected through test spraying, and blockages require nozzle replacement, which cannot be cleaned on-site immediately.

Currently, Sames offers three main types of Airmix guns:

* Xcite 120 (up to 120 bar) – primarily used in woodworking or for low-filled, low-viscosity coatings
* Xcite 240 (up to 240 bar) – commonly used in heavy machinery and steel structure manufacturing, as it can quickly, economically, and neatly cover large surfaces
* Xcite 400 (up to 400 bar)
