### **TEC5 – A New Approach to Powder Output Control**

The first innovation is a powder-delivery regulation method that has not yet become widespread in the market. This is Sames’ TEC5 – Transfer Electric Control system, the fifth step in a development process spanning 55 years.

In manual powder coating, coating defects often arise simply from changes in the distance between the spray gun and the workpiece.

* When the distance increases, particle charge decreases, which leads to weaker attraction, poor coverage, and surface defects.
* When the distance becomes too small, current spikes rapidly. Beyond posing a safety risk for the operator, it also degrades the coating quality.

The Sames INOBOX control unit actively compensates for these issues. Within a working distance of 5–30 cm, it automatically adjusts the voltage and current levels to ensure a stable powder flow, delivering a consistent deposit on the part.

### **A Breakthrough Innovation in Progress: "Powder-on-Powder" Application**

The second development is an ongoing innovation supported by DEWA Zrt., which supplies equipment to PTG Kft., the developer of the process.

The owner of PTG Kft., András Dudás, is working on introducing a coating method that would be groundbreaking not only in Hungary but potentially worldwide: the “powder-on-powder” coating process, similar in concept to liquid-on-liquid painting.

The idea is to apply two different powder layers—for example, a primer and a topcoat—in one continuous process, followed by a single curing cycle.

Sames is the only manufacturer on the Hungarian market fully supporting this development and supplying the necessary equipment:

* two powder guns with different charging technologies,
* and the required powder feed and control units.

Why two guns?

Traditional powder coating uses a negatively charged cascade. Negative powder cannot be applied on top of another negative powder layer due to charge repulsion.

Therefore:

* the first layer is applied using positive charging,
* the second layer is applied using negative charging.

This ensures that both layers adhere correctly and fuse into a uniform coating during curing.

This method not only has the potential to cut coating time in half, but because only one curing cycle is needed, it also offers significant energy savings.
