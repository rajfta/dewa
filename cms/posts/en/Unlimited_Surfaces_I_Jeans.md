Jeans production actually has a significant history in Hungary as well, with several renowned international brands taking part over the decades. Back in the Kádár era, the May 1st Clothing Factory — which in the 1970s was one of Europe’s largest apparel manufacturers — signed a cooperation agreement with the world-famous Levi Strauss company in 1977. As a result, original Levi’s jeans began rolling off the production lines in the Marcali plant in the summer of 1978.

Over the years, about 800,000 pairs of jeans were produced annually, 60% of which were purchased back by Levi Strauss, while 40% remained available on the domestic market. In the late 1980s, Levi Strauss moved its production to Kiskunhalas, and in 1992, the Marcali plant was taken over by the German denim brand Mustang. Mustang continued production there until March 2008, when rising costs led to the end of jeans manufacturing in Hungary.

But what role did Dewa play in this story?

![](/famergy%C3%A1rt%C3%A1s1.jpg)

In 2003, our company supplied specialized paint booths and spray systems to the Marcali factory, and the following year to the Kiskunhalas plant as well. These installations played a key role in giving jeans their distinctive look — this is where the manual acid washing, etching, and dyeing took place. Thanks to these technologies, Levi’s and Mustang jeans could achieve that characteristically worn, unique appearance that customers around the world loved so much.

So, perhaps surprisingly, we can say that Dewa also contributed — in our own way — to the global success of jeans.
